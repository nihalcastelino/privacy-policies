1. Scope and controller

This policy applies to the Global Research Nexus mobile applications for iOS and Android, the Global Research Nexus website, and the connected Global Research Nexus service. Global Research Nexus is responsible for the processing described here. Questions and rights requests can be sent to privacy@researchnexus.in.

Your Google Scholar profile

If you add a link to your Google Scholar profile, GRN reads that public profile so it can show your publications, citations and h-index on your GRN profile. To read it, GRN uses a search provider (SerpAPI), because Google Scholar does not offer its own way to do this. GRN reads only what is already public on your Scholar page: your name, institution, the email domain Scholar shows as verified, research topics, co-authors, papers and citation counts.

Before showing these numbers to other members, GRN checks that the Scholar profile is yours, comparing it with your GRN profile: your name, institution, role, research interests, and the domain of your email address (for example "soa.ac.in", never the full address). When the match isn't clear, an automated AI check compares the same details and decides whether the two profiles are the same person. Its answer is stored for up to 90 days so the same comparison isn't repeated. If GRN can't confirm the profile is yours, the numbers stay visible only to you, and we tell you what would confirm it.

If you confirm a work email, GRN stores it privately to check your institution; only its domain is used for matching, and you can remove it at any time.

Your public profile (optional)

Once GRN has checked who you are — your professional registration or your institution, not only an ORCID link — you can choose to make a short public profile, for example to link from your email signature. It is off unless you turn it on, in Profile on the web or Professional details in the app. It stops working if that check lapses or if you change your name: you can turn it on again 30 days after a name change, and a page that says “Verified by GRN” needs GRN to check your new name first. When it is on, anyone with its link (theresearchnexus.in/r/your-name) can see your name, your institution if GRN has verified your affiliation, what GRN checked (“Verified by GRN” only where we checked your registration or verified you ourselves; otherwise “Affiliation confirmed”), your job title as your own description, your paper, citation and h-index figures if they come from a Google Scholar profile GRN has confirmed is yours or from an ORCID record you signed in with (always with their source and date), and the links you added. It never shows your email address, your posts, who you follow, your messages or your activity. People you have blocked can still see it if they are signed out. We ask search engines not to index it. You can turn it off at any time in the same place; the page then stops working straight away. If you change your link, the old one forwards to the new one while your profile is on (you can stop that), and no one else can ever take a link that has been your public address. If you combine two GRN accounts, links from the account you combine away forward to the profile of the account you keep. Anyone can report a public profile to GRN from the page; a report does not take it down by itself, and for a report we keep only a one-way fingerprint of the sender’s network address, for 30 days. If GRN takes a page offline after a report, it stays off until GRN reviews it again.

Numbers shown to others are labelled as coming from your Google Scholar profile. You can remove the link at any time; GRN then stops showing those numbers and deletes what it read. If you use "Find my Google Scholar profile", GRN searches Google Scholar for your name only when you ask it to.

3. Why we use data

  • Authenticate accounts and maintain sessions.
  • Display professional profiles and provide research discovery, events and funding information.
  • Show publication and citation figures from a Google Scholar profile you link, after checking that it is yours.
  • Build a feed from followed researchers and sources.
  • Publish user-requested posts and images and notify relevant followers in-app or through optional push notifications.
  • Save follows, funding deadlines, RSVPs and policy choices.
  • Prevent spam, abuse, impersonation and unlawful content through reports, blocks and moderation.
  • Meet legal obligations, resolve disputes and protect the service.

Depending on where you live, processing is based on providing the service you request, your consent, our legitimate interests in operating a safe professional network, and legal obligations.

4. Service providers and sharing

We use Supabase to provide authentication, database, file storage and server-side functions. Expo and Firebase process device push tokens and delivery status solely to deliver optional notifications. PostHog processes anonymous product-analytics events for Global Research Nexus, in its European region; we do not enable session recording and we send it no advertising identifier. SerpAPI reads the public Google Scholar profile you link, and runs a Google Scholar name search only when you use "Find my Google Scholar profile". An AI model provider powers GRN's AI features, including the check that a linked Google Scholar profile is yours; for that check it receives the public details described above and your email domain only, never your full email address, and it processes them only to give GRN its answer. An email delivery provider sends GRN's emails, including sign-in links and verification codes. Supabase, PostHog, SerpAPI, our AI model provider and our email delivery provider process data for Global Research Nexus as service providers. We may also disclose limited data when required by law, to protect users and the service, or during a legitimate organisational transaction subject to appropriate safeguards.

We do not sell personal data. We do not share personal data with advertising networks and do not use behavioural advertising.

5. Cookies and tracking

We do not use cookies to track you, and we do not track you by any other means. We do not collect an advertising identifier, we show no advertising, we use no advertising, attribution or data-broker software, and we do not share personal data with anyone for tracking or advertising purposes. Nothing we collect is combined with data from other companies' apps or websites.

The mobile apps contain no embedded web browser. When you open a journal article, a funding call or another external link, it opens in your device's own browser, and any cookies that site sets are that site's and are governed by its own privacy policy, not ours.

Signing in with ORCID opens your device's secure sign-in window. That window is a private session: it is not shared with your browser and it is discarded when sign-in finishes. Cookies inside it exist only to make the sign-in work.

The website uses a small number of strictly necessary cookies, and no others: a session cookie that keeps you signed in, a short-lived security cookie that protects the ORCID sign-in from cross-site request forgery, and a cookie that records that you chose to open the read-only public demo. None of them identifies you to any third party, and none is used for analytics or advertising.

6. Visibility and your choices

Profile information and content you publish may be visible to signed-in users or followers according to the feature used. Reports and block relationships are private safety data. You can unfollow sources, remove saved activity, disable event or network notifications, deny notification permission in system settings, block users, report content, sign out and request account deletion. Do not publish confidential research, patient information or content you do not have permission to share.

7. Retention and deletion

Account and feature data is kept while your account is active and as needed to provide the service. When deletion is completed, account-linked profile, network, saved activity, posts, notifications and uploaded images are deleted or de-identified. Anonymised moderation and security records may be retained for up to 90 days to prevent abuse and document enforcement, then deleted. Search history keeps your newest 200 searches; older ones fall off, and nothing is kept on a timer, so a search stays until you delete it or newer ones push it out. You can delete any search, or all of them, at any time. Figures read from a linked Google Scholar profile are kept while the link stays on your profile; removing or changing the link deletes them, and our cached copy of that Scholar page is deleted once no GRN member links it. The answer of the automated identity check is kept for up to 90 days. Backups may expire on their normal protected rotation and are not used to restore a deleted account.

See the Account and Data Deletion page for in-app and external request routes.

8. Security and international processing

We use encrypted network connections, row-level database access controls, authenticated server functions, private media storage and least-privilege administrative access. No internet service can guarantee absolute security. Supabase infrastructure may process data outside your country under its contractual and legal safeguards.

9. Your rights

Depending on your location, you may have rights to access, correct, export, restrict, object to or delete personal data, and to withdraw consent where consent is the basis. Email privacy@researchnexus.in from the address associated with your account. We may verify your identity before acting.
