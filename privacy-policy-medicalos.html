1. Who we are and what this covers

MedicalOS is an anatomy learning application for medical students. This policy explains what we collect, why, who else handles it, and what you can do about it. It is written to meet the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (India).

It covers every way of using MedicalOS:

  • the mobile apps for iPhone and iPad (App Store) and for Android (Google Play);
  • the web application;
  • MedicalOS for Windows, the desktop app, whether you installed it from the Microsoft Store or with the installer downloaded from GitHub.

2.7 Technical data

  • IP address and request logs. Our servers log each request: the time, the address it was made from, the path, and the result. Some security and audit entries include your account identifier. We use them to keep the service secure, to limit abuse, and to find faults.
  • Rate limits. To stop abuse and runaway AI spend, counters keyed on your account identifier or IP address are kept for short periods.
  • Update checks. The mobile apps ask Expo for app updates, and the installer version of the Windows app asks GitHub for new versions. Both see your IP address and the version you run, as any download does. Neither receives your account details.

2.8 If you are a lecturer or contributor

If you write teaching content or send messages to classes, we also keep what you author (topics, figures, edits and their history), your public byline (display name, affiliation and honorific, if you set them), the comments you leave on colleagues’ topics, the messages you send to classes (students see them as from “Your instructor”, and we keep the message and who sent it), the classes you run and their rosters, and a record of who changed or approved what, because other people’s learning depends on that record. Lecturers also see the other people’s data described in section 5.

2.9 What stays on your device

  • Phone. Your sign-in session, a copy of your progress, the lessons downloaded for offline study (removed when you sign out), your random installation identifier, and your push token.
  • Web. The same kinds of data in the browser’s own storage: your sign-in session, a random device identifier, your progress and the lessons you are reading, which are removed when you sign out. We do not set cookies.
  • Windows. Your sign-in session is stored in an encrypted file in the app’s data folder. The key that encrypts it, your device identifier and your installation identifier are kept in Windows Credential Manager. Signing out deletes the encrypted session. Uninstalling the Microsoft Store version removes its files and the web data it keeps, but Windows leaves the three Credential Manager entries (named org.medicalos.desktop.store for the Store version and org.medicalos.desktop for the installer version); with the files gone they protect nothing, and you can delete them in Windows Credential Manager. 3. What we do not collect

So that there is no ambiguity, and because app stores ask us to be specific:

  • No location data. The apps do not ask for location permission. Our servers see your IP address (section 2.7) but we do not use it to work out where you are.
  • No advertising identifier. MedicalOS contains no advertising and no advertising SDK.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDK, and no cross-app or cross-site tracking. (Apple and Google run the push services that deliver notifications to your phone, and Expo delivers app updates; section 7.)
  • No camera, microphone, photo or contact access on the mobile apps, and no access to your calendar. On the web and in the Windows app, a lecturer can choose a file to upload (for example a Word document or an image) and only that file is read.
  • No health or fitness data. What you study is education, not a record about your body.
  • No financial or payment information. MedicalOS is free to use.

4. How we use it, and why

  • To run the app — signing you in, storing your progress and answers, scheduling your revision, and keeping your phone, web and computer in step.
  • To run classes and lectures — letting lecturers manage a class, run a live lecture, take attendance and send messages, and letting you receive them.
  • To mark your work — checking your answers and working out your mastery of each concept, with AI where an answer cannot be marked by matching alone.
  • To protect the service and its content — limiting each account to a few devices, spotting a shared login or a shared screenshot, limiting abuse and AI spend, and investigating faults.
  • To keep the app current — knowing which versions are installed so that we can fix problems and retire versions that are no longer supported.
  • To meet legal obligations — and to answer your requests and grievances.

We process your data with your consent, which you give by creating an account and using the features described here, and which you can withdraw at any time by deleting your account. We also process some data where the law allows it without consent, such as keeping the service secure and meeting legal obligations. 6. Sign-in

Depending on the app you can sign in with Google, with Apple, or with a one-time link sent to your email address. There is no password to set or forget. When you use Google or Apple, they tell us your email address and, if you allow it, your name; our sign-in provider may also hold other details the provider shares, such as a profile picture address, that MedicalOS does not use. We do not receive your password.

If you use Apple’s Hide My Email, Apple gives us a relay address instead of your own. That works normally, but it is a different address from your Google or email sign-in, so it creates a separate account with separate progress.

10. Your rights

Under the DPDPA you may ask us for a summary of the personal data we hold about you and who we share it with, ask us to correct or complete it, ask us to erase it, withdraw your consent, use our grievance process, and nominate another person to exercise these rights for you if you die or become unable to. Deletion is available in the app at any time and needs no request — see section 11 and the deletion page. For anything else, email nihal@cloudzzy.co.uk from the address on your account. We respond within 30 days.

You can also see and sign out your own devices from inside the app, switch lecturer-message notifications off, and turn off push notifications in your phone’s settings. If you are not satisfied with our answer, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

11. Deleting your account

Delete your account from the app (phone: More, then Delete account; web and Windows: Profile, then Delete account). Deleting removes your sign-in and profile, your progress, review cards, answers, mastery and activity records, your class and institution memberships, your lecture attendance, questions and poll answers, your inbox and notification settings, your push tokens and notification records, your devices, installation records and screenshot reports, your invitation records, and every class invitation sent to your email address. If you are a lecturer, the classes you own are removed with you, and your authored topics are withheld while rights over them are resolved.

Some records survive without your name. AI usage records stay with the link to your account removed. Records of who edited or approved teaching content stay with your name removed. Our access-audit log, which records class and institution actions, can keep the random account identifier of a deleted account, which no longer points to any person. If you authored teaching content, a restricted record of ownership and permissions is kept for up to 90 days, then deleted unless the law requires us to keep it. Backups, server logs and the sign-in provider’s event log are covered by section 9. The deletion page lists what is removed in full.

12. Children

MedicalOS is built for medical students aged 18 and over and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you believe a child has created an account, email us and we will remove it.

13. Security

Data is encrypted in transit. Access to the database is restricted by row-level security and by the checks our server makes on who is asking, so that a student can read only their own data and a lecturer only what section 5 describes. On Windows, your sign-in session is encrypted on the computer with a key held in Windows Credential Manager. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach puts personal data at risk, we will tell affected users and the Data Protection Board of India without undue delay.

14. Changes

If we change this policy we will update the date at the top and, for material changes, tell you in the app. Continuing to use MedicalOS after a change means the updated policy applies.
